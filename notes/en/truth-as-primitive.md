---
title: Truth as a primitive
date: 2026-10-03
kind: Working note
status: Working thesis
---

Truth cannot be defined through other concepts in the very language in which it is being defined, provided that the language can name its own sentences and substitute a sentence's name into a formula, and its underlying logic is classical or intuitionistic.

A definition of truth that claims to capture the concept already in use is itself an assertion, and the claim to truth lies in the very force of assertion: truth is the measure of correctness of what is asserted. The measure of a language's assertions extends to all of its assertions. So a definition of our truth is a definition of truth for the very language in which it is stated, and it must cover all of that language's sentences, including sentences about the definition itself.

But in such a language, for any candidate D one can construct a sentence that says of itself that D does not apply to it, and D gets that sentence wrong (Tarski). So every definition either errs on its own language or holds only on a fragment from which some sentences about the definition itself have been removed - and those sentences are no longer judged by it.

For a system's own provability the result is even harsher: a formal system that proves that everything provable in it is true (Pr(⌜φ⌝) → φ for every φ) proves everything (Löb).

Hence truth cannot be defined by any means of the language in which it serves as the measure. It is a semantic primitive as a position: in its own language, the undefinable measure of assertion; in the metalanguage, a definable object, while the measure of the metalanguage itself is, there, once again undefinable.
